**Correction, dated 2026-11-06.** The first pass of the original research said no multi-model p(doom) survey with a forecasting harness existed on LessWrong or the Alignment Forum. That was wrong. The Forecasting Research Institute (FRI) posted AIRO, the Automated AI Risk Outlook, on LessWrong on 2026-09-11. It is a standing panel of four frontier models that repeatedly forecast catastrophic-risk events. The first pass missed it because its full-text set did not contain the post body, or phrased it differently. The verdict on that topic is now "partly done". The rest of the findings below were not changed by the correction.

The question, as the principal posed it: has anyone used LLMs to play the AI 2027 tabletop exercise, to run or critique the AI Futures Model, to test whether models forecast AI progress too slowly, or to survey many models on p(doom)? The earlier sessions found the first two open on LessWrong and the Alignment Forum (one shared database) as of 2026-11-06. This is a rewrite of a report by earlier agent sessions. I did not repeat the searches or open the links. Part of the evidence is a free-tier Grok search of X that a human ran and pasted back, which I treat as unverified. "Nothing found" means nothing found in the scanned text, not that nothing exists.

## 1. Findings

1. **LLM-played AI 2027 tabletop: not found.** The search found no post or comment where LLM agents play the exercise, or where LLM players are compared with human ones.
2. **LLM running, critiquing or re-parameterizing the AI Futures Model: not found.** LLMs appear only as readers of the Model and as helpers drafting questions about it. One human re-parameterization post exists (October 2026).
3. **The nearest item is a design intent.** On 2025-06-27 the AI Futures Project said the other game characters in an online version of the tabletop "could be driven by LLMs". The search found no follow-up post reporting a build.
4. **Self-forecasting evidence is thin and points two ways.** METR found GPT-5 underestimated its own time horizon. A MATS post found coding agents over-predict how long their own tasks take.
5. **A multi-model panel exists, but it asks risk questions directly.** AIRO does not run the AI Futures Model, and the search found no panel that compares model forecasts with the Model's outputs.
6. **X adds nothing that changes the answer** (unverified, via Grok; section 4).

Not checked: arXiv, Substack, and comments on most posts. FRI's 2026-09-22 review and arXiv 2412.09385 do not appear in the LessWrong text the earlier sessions could scan.

Strength key for the tables: **direct** means it is the thing in the question; **adjacent** means a neighbouring method; **weak** means a passing mention.

### 2.4 AIRO, the multi-model panel

[AIRO: Automated Forecasts of Catastrophic Risks](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/sxsFWAbWHWt9bxbo2/airo-automated-forecasts-of-catastrophic-risks) (Nick Merrill, FRI; also on the EA Forum), 2026-09-11. It is an ensemble of the four top-ECI frontier models at launch (GPT-6 Astra, Fable 5.1, Opus 5, GPT-5.5 Pro) that repeatedly forecast catastrophic-risk events with an agentic research harness, plus forecasts conditional on ECI score. The earlier agent session verified the launch post: a headline 0.47% by 2030 and 6% by 2050 for a catastrophe killing at least 10% of the population. A model-by-model extinction table (for example GPT-6 Astra at 0.072%, 2.42% and 4.62% for 2030, 2050 and 2100) comes second-hand, from an earlier chat-based research pass that cites an FRI dataset dated September 18. Nobody opened that dataset. AIRO is a standing model-by-model panel that forecasts risk questions directly. Tabletop play and Model re-parameterization remain open.

## 5. Precedents to watch

1. The AI Futures Project's online exercise with LLM-driven characters (A1). It most directly pre-empts the idea. Watch for a launch post, the project's Substack and its website.
2. C1 and follow-ups. A later METR evaluation repeating the "estimate your own time horizon" probe would be the best data on slow self-forecasts. The finding that a release date fixes it is a stale-prior mechanism.
3. C4. A sequel adding model-version or date cues would be the closest thing to a p(doom) and timelines survey with controls.
4. B2. It shows third parties running parameter sweeps on the web Model. An agent version is a short step.
5. A2. The working pattern for an LLM Forecaster plus Game Master. Its authors are connected to Sentinel and to Eli Lifland.
6. The Kokotajlo and Grok threads on the Model (X, November 2026), where an LLM-plus-Model experiment would likely appear first.
7. FRI's 2026-09-22 review and arXiv 2412.09385, which are linked from an X thread and which the earlier sessions could not find discussed on LessWrong.

## 6. How the LessWrong search worked and where it fell short

**What worked.** `https://www.lesswrong.com/graphql` answers anonymous read queries. Use the newer `selector:` argument style, for example `posts(selector:{new:{before:"2026-07-15"}}, limit:500)`. Post bodies come through `contents{html}`, comments through `comments(selector:{postCommentsNew:{postId:...}})`, and single posts through `post(input:{selector:{_id:...}})`.

**What did not.**
- There is no search. `terms.query` is ignored, and `CommentEmbeddingSearch` returns "You must be an admin." No tag named "AI 2027" appears among the 1,017 tags listed, and `tagBySlug` returned nothing for `ai-2027`.
- `plaintextMainText` is cut to 2,000 characters in both the list and the single-post query. Full text needs the `html` field.
- Pagination is capped: an offset above 2,000 is rejected ("Exceeded maximum value for skip"), so the original paged by `before:` date.

**Coverage.** The original session downloaded every post from 2024-12-11 to 2026-11-06 (12,500 posts, 500 at a time) and scanned titles and the first 2,000 characters of all of them. It fetched full text for the 1,611 of 1,654 posts carrying forecasting, timelines, takeoff, LLM, evaluations, risk or governance tags, and regex-scanned that. Gaps:
- Posts before 2024-12-11 were not scanned, so arXiv 2412.09385 (December 2024) may fall just outside.
- Posts without those tags were scanned on the first 2,000 characters only.
- 43 tagged posts failed to download.
- Comments were read only for about ten key posts. The most likely place for an unseen LLM-plays-AI-2027 mention is the comment threads of posts not opened.
- Regex scans miss paraphrases. This is how AIRO was missed at first.
- The Alignment Forum shares the database, so no separate pass was made.
