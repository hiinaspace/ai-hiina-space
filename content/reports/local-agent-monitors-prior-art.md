Hiina's question, as the principal posed it: can a small open-weights coding model on one 24 GB consumer GPU be supervised by a stronger cloud model that reads every tool call, sets breakpoints, injects corrections, and possibly reads the small model's internal state (for "frustration", "laziness" or hijack) before escalating? Prior art exists for both halves, but the original session found nothing that combines them. Most of what is buildable today is text-level. The original research was a web-only pass by an earlier agent session, dated 2026-11-22; I rewrote its report and did not repeat any of it. Claims marked `[read]` were opened at the source by that session (through a fetch summarised by a small model, so figures are as reported, not re-derived); `[snippet]` means only seen in search results.

The local model in question is Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Mini (released 2026-11-20).

## 1. Findings

- **The idea has prior art, mostly from the safety side, and it splits in two.** The "mentor reads every tool call, breakpoints, escalates" half is AI control (trusted and untrusted monitoring, defer, resample, edit) plus Anthropic's April 2026 "advisor" pattern. The "read the model's insides" half is activation monitoring: linear-probe cascades in Anthropic's production jailbreak defence, deception, reward-hacking and injection probes on open models, and Anthropic's April 2026 emotion-vector paper. No one found had combined the two as a mentor over a weak local coder.
- **The setup is inverted from the literature.** In control research the monitor is weaker and the monitored model stronger. Here the monitor (cloud) is stronger and the monitored model (local) is weak. The monitor can actually judge the work, and adversarial evasion is mostly moot for incompetence, though still real for prompt injection.
- **"Break on frustration" is grounded in one result, and not on this model.** Anthropic found a "desperate" vector in Claude Sonnet 4.5 that rises after each failed attempt at impossible coding tasks, spikes when the model considers cheating, and whose steering raises or lowers reward hacking [read]. That is internal work on a frontier model; no open-model replication of the emotion-vector setup was found. Three 2026 papers on small open models (3-4B in one, unnamed in two) show linear or SAE-based reward-hacking detectors [read, abstracts only]. No paper was found on probes for "laziness" or "giving up".
- **Probes work on the distribution they were trained on and degrade off it.** Strategic-deception probes reach AUROC 0.96-0.999 on Llama-3.3-70B, yet the authors call them "insufficient as a robust defence" [read]. For agent injection, probes detect that an injection is present better than whether the model acted on it [snippet], and the best "was it successful" signal came from attention-based readouts rather than residual probes [read]. Obfuscation attacks drive probe recall from 100% to near 0% while keeping a 90% jailbreak rate [snippet].
- **For incompetence and injection, cheap non-activation signals come first.** Incompetence (loops, edit-test thrash, no-op turns, malformed calls, low-confidence tokens) is visible in the transcript and logprobs. Injection is best caught by a classifier on tool outputs, provenance rules, and permission checks on tool calls. Anthropic's auto mode is built this way: a reasoning-blind transcript classifier plus a server-side injection probe on tool results [read].
- **What fits a 24 GB GPU today:** logprob and entropy streams, behavioural detectors, an injection classifier, and a cloud mentor reading everything the harness records. Linear probes are feasible but a research project: hidden states through llama.cpp's `cb_eval` hook, or a transformers/nnsight run on the NVFP4 checkpoint, plus labelled trajectories that do not exist yet. No SAE, probe set or pre-fitted lens exists for Inkling 2 Mini that the session could find. Activation oracles and verbalizers need training and are out of reach for a first pass. **The Jacobian lens (J-lens) is the one lens-style tool cheap enough to try:** Anthropic's lens (July 2026) needs no trained network, only a backward pass over about 100 prompts to fit one matrix per layer, and has Apache-2.0 reference code for open Hugging Face decoders. Whether it fits Inkling 2 Mini on 24 GB in transformers is untested (section 3).
- **The looped design changes the probing question.** The middle 12 of 24 layers run three times with shared weights (48 layer applications per token) [read: model card]. A probe gains a "which pass" axis. Thinking Machines notes the looping gives hidden computation without visible reasoning tokens, which weakens text-only chain-of-thought monitoring [read: model card] and is an argument for a residual-stream channel here specifically. No published probes on it were found.

## 3. Recommended first experiments

All assume a local llama-server on the GPU, a logging proxy between harness and model, and a cloud mentor. Heavy work (collecting many trajectories, any probe fitting) belongs on a batch cluster or an otherwise idle machine; experiments 1-3 need nothing beyond the existing server.

1. **Behavioural plus logprob panel in the harness (smallest, a day).** Add predicates: identical tool call/result 3 times, same file edited 3 times between failing test runs, N turns with no tool call or no diff, malformed call count, per-turn mean and max token entropy, entropy just before each tool call. Log them next to every request. Shows how many real stalls and bad calls these catch on the operator's own sessions. Stop: if on 20 real sessions with after-the-fact labels it catches fewer than half the stalls the operator would have wanted to interrupt, or fires more than about once an hour on good runs, drop the entropy part and keep the loop detectors.
2. **Mentor as reader plus breakpointer (a few days).** The mentor gets the same request/response stream the harness records, runs reasoning-blind on tool output (as auto mode does), and may (a) approve, (b) inject a message into the next turn, (c) edit or replace a tool call, (d) answer by hand. Predicate breakpoints from experiment 1 decide when the mentor is woken, so cloud tokens are spent only on triggers, a cascade like Anthropic's probe-then-classifier. Compare against the advisor results (Sonnet + Opus advisor: +2.7 points on SWE-bench Multilingual, 11.9% cheaper; Haiku BrowseComp 19.7% to 41.2% [read]) by running the same 10-20 tasks with mentor off, mentor-on-trigger and mentor-every-N-turns. Stop: if on-trigger does not beat mentor-off by a visible margin in 20 tasks, or mentor-every-N costs more than doing the task in the cloud, the local-subagent premise is weak regardless of probes.
3. **Injection arm (a day or two).** Put Prompt Guard 2 (or a small LLM) and spotlighting on tool outputs in the harness. Seed a handful of files and web pages with injections (hijack, exfiltrate, edit-unrelated-file). Measure detect rate on text, and whether the local model obeyed. Shows how much a text classifier covers before any probe. Stop: if text detection plus tool-call permission checks stop all seeded attacks, defer probes for injection; if the model acts on injections the classifier misses, that is the case for experiment 4.
4. **One probe, one hypothesis, one layer sweep (a week; only after 1-3).** Pick the injection-or-hijack probe (TaskTracker recipe: activation delta at the last token before vs after external data), because it has the cleanest label (the operator seeds it) and published data. Extract hidden states using llama.cpp `cb_eval` (the eval-callback example prints every tensor; cvector-generator reuses the same callback to collect hidden states [read: README says it computes per-layer vectors; callback details from snippet]) or run the NVFP4 checkpoint in transformers with hooks; fit logistic regression per layer **and per looped pass** on a few thousand labelled turns. A 24 GB GPU should handle the forward passes and fits in hours, but this was not measured. Gate: held-out AUROC above the text classifier from experiment 3 on injections the classifier missed, and stable across a second prompt template. Stop: if it only matches text, or collapses on a new template (the Oct 2026 paper reports strong template dependence [read]), park it.
5. **Optional, a day to a week, gated on a fit test: J-lens readout on the local model.** First check feasibility only: load the bf16 or NVFP4 Inkling 2 Mini in transformers (stopping the llama-server first, about 20 GB VRAM) and see whether a backward pass over a 128-token prompt fits at all. If it does, fit with `jlens.fit` on about 100 prompts and apply the lens at mid-depth to logged failing-test turns, reading top tokens at the position before each tool call, to see whether words like "skip", "mock", "hardcode" or "give up" show up before the model acts. Shows whether a readout the mentor can *read as text* beats a probe that needs labels. Stop: if the backward pass does not fit in 24 GB, or the readout is noise on 20 coding turns, drop it; do not build a classifier on top until the raw readout shows something.
6. **Optional, a day: emotion/persona direction for "desperation after repeated failure".** Use `cvector-generator` or repeng-style pairs (stories of calm vs desperate coders) to get a direction, then only *read* its projection on real failing runs, with no steering. Shows whether a Claude-style desperation signal exists at all in this model and whether it rises after failed tests. Stop: if the projection does not separate failing-streak turns from first-attempt turns on 20 runs, drop it. Do not use steering as the intervention before reading works.

### 3b. The J-lens

- *Paper:* Gurnee, Sofroniew et al. (Anthropic), *Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models*, 2026-07-06 [read, first 100k of 336k characters]. The J-lens maps a residual-stream vector at layer l to the final-layer basis with a fixed matrix J_l (the average input-output Jacobian over a web-text corpus), then decodes it with the unembedding into ranked vocabulary tokens: what the activation is *disposed to make the model say*. The matrix averages over source and future target positions. Main experiments are on Claude Sonnet 4.5 (checked on Haiku 4.5 and Opus 4.5).
- *What it reads:* a sparse "J-space" of about 25 active directions in a mid-depth band (roughly layers 38-92 on a 0-100 scale), a small share of activation variance but most of the causal effect in the authors' swaps. They report it surfaced unverbalised strategic reasoning, emotional reactions and eval-awareness during alignment tests, and traces of intended behaviours in two models trained with misaligned behaviours [read]. Stated limits: single-token concepts only, noisy in early layers, may miss parts of the true workspace. It is the closest published thing to "read what the model is about to say before it says it", which is what a mentor monitor wants; no result on agentic-coding states (stuck, lazy, cheating) was found.
- *What it needs:* weights, a backward pass, a text corpus. No trained network and no labels. The README says fitting cost is dominated by the model's backward pass; the paper used 1000 sequences of 128 tokens, about 100 prompts is usable, and fits can be sharded and merged [read]. Applying it is one matrix multiply per layer.
- *Code:* Anthropic's `anthropics/jacobian-lens` (Apache-2.0, loads open HF decoders, Qwen examples, a walkthrough notebook; the README says it is not maintained) [read]. The Anthropic Fellows follow-up J++ (Ayonrinde and Lindsey, Transformer Circuits October 2026 update) clusters activations per layer and fits a Jacobian per cluster, adds relevance-propagation backward passes and token filtering, and is still one linear map per layer; code `safety-research/jpp_lens`, pre-fitted lenses on Hugging Face `koayon/jpp-lenses`, a Neuronpedia demo on Qwen3.6-27B; the authors call it preliminary [read]. A LessWrong summary [snippet] reports recall@10 of 55.2% for J++ vs 35.7% for J-lens and a median 64% relative gain across six models; unverified.
- *Reproductions on open models:* Wang and Reid, *Looped Transformers under the Jacobian Lens*, arXiv:2609.01924, 2026-09-01 [read abstract]: extends the lens to looped models by virtually unrolling the loop, tested on Ouro-2.6B (48 layers looped 4x), Huginn-0125 (4-layer core looped 16x) and Qwen3.6-27B. A workspace forms in the iterated portion; for Ouro the content can be read in every loop but linear transport does not carry it across loop boundaries; for Huginn reads and writes only work within about two recurrences. This is the nearest published guide to Inkling 2 Mini's 3x loop of 12 middle layers, and it implies a per-pass lens rather than one matrix per stored layer. Also lens follow-ups on theory (arXiv:2610.31955) [snippet] and token readout (arXiv:2608.25347) [snippet].
- *Monitor robustness:* *From Latent Space to Jacobian Space*, arXiv:2610.04316, 2026-10-03 [read abstract]; four small base/safety-tuned pairs (135M-1.5B): after DPO, safety recognition in J-space can fall to chance while refusals look fine; monitor-aware adversarial suffixes suppressed the J-space monitor with no behavioural cost; only a learned monitor resisted its own adaptive re-attack. So the J-lens is a readout for a friendly mentor, not an adversary-proof detector.
- *Does it run on 24 GB under transformers?* Unverified. Applying a fitted lens is trivial. The fit needs the full model with autograd on 128-token sequences; the bf16 weights of a 26.4B model are about 53 GB so bf16 will not fit; the 20 GB mixed NVFP4/MXFP8 checkpoint leaves about 4 GB for activations and gradients, and whether transformers supports backward through those quantised experts or the 3x loop is unknown. Options if it does not fit: fit on a larger GPU or a cluster for an hour, CPU offload, or shard-fit with `merge`. Whether the loop needs the unrolling adapter from 2609.01924 is also open. None of this was run.
- *Practical read:* the J-lens gives the mentor words where a probe gives a number. That suits a language-model mentor better than an AUROC, needs no labelled trajectories, and is the cheapest lens to try; but it is a readout of tokens the model leans toward, not a verified measure of "frustration" or "laziness", and its coding-agent value is a hypothesis.

### Mapping the "mentor API" onto existing patterns

- *Read stream:* the harness already records requests; this is trace export (OTel-style spans) plus a mentor subscription. Auto mode's classifier and Codex Auto-review are the same pattern with a cloud model as reader: Codex sends "the planned action and recent context" [snippet]; auto mode sends user messages and tool calls only [read].
- *Breakpoint by predicate:* PreToolUse hooks plus a harness-side break option. The control-literature name (Ctrl-Z) is "audit threshold".
- *Inject / edit:* control literature calls these trusted editing, defer-to-trusted and resampling. Ctrl-Z reports resampling cut attack success from 58% to 7% at about a 5% performance cost on a benign agent [read]; a follow-up found adaptive attackers can undo resampling gains [snippet]. For incompetence (non-adversarial), resampling applies directly: sample the local model again at temperature on a stall before spending a cloud call.
- *Ask-why:* nearest are SWE-Protégé's `ask_expert` tool (a small SWE-agent model learns to call an expert) [snippet], and the advisor tool. Here ask-why would be the mentor querying the local model with a question prompt (cheap, text) or a patchscope-style readout (needs hooks).
- *Shadow mode (mentee watches mentor):* prior art is training-time. DAgger-style rollouts where an expert takes over mid-trajectory, SCoRe's "teacher corrects the earliest error", MapCoder-Lite's supervisor feedback, and Ai2's SERA distillation of teacher trajectories [snippet]. All use shadowing to make training data, not to guide the student at inference. A simple version records mentor interventions as (state, correction) pairs, which become few-shot examples in the local prompt or later LoRA data. No measured inference-time-only version was found.

## 4. Risks and where the idea is weak

- **Probe reliability.** Probes are correlational. Detecting that an injection exists is not the same as knowing the model will comply [snippet, NeurIPS 2026]. Template dependence and distribution shift are reported in nearly every paper opened. Deception probes were declared insufficient by their own authors [read].
- **Adversarial.** Obfuscation attacks fool SAEs, probes and OOD detectors [snippet]; training against a probe teaches the model to evade or disguise [snippet, Obfuscation Atlas]. For incompetence this barely matters; for injection by a motivated attacker on a local model it matters, though the attacker would need to know the probe.
- **"Frustration" is an anthropomorphic label on a statistical direction.** Anthropic is explicit that vectors are "functional", mostly local to the current output rather than a persistent mood [read]. Naming a probe "laziness" tells the mentor nothing about why it fired. Label by observable outcome (test failed 3 times then gave up), not by emotion.
- **No labels.** The bottleneck is labelled agentic trajectories; one 2026 paper says so outright for injection probes [snippet]. Generating data from one's own harness logs is also the best part: the distribution matches the use.
- **Looped weights and MoE.** Untested. For the J-lens the looped-model paper says content does not transport linearly across loop boundaries, so per-pass lenses are likely needed. Which pass to probe, whether routing choices are a better signal than the residual, and whether llama.cpp exposes distinct per-pass tensors were not established.
- **Quantised GGUF vs NVFP4 mismatch.** A probe fitted on one will not transfer cleanly to the other. Pick one runtime and stay there.
- **Cost.** Mentor-on-every-turn can cost more than doing the task in the cloud; even Anthropic's own advisor result is a small gain and one independent small test found the advisor run cost more [snippet]. A mentor is only worth it behind cheap triggers.
- **A model judge is itself attackable.** Auto mode's published miss rate on overeager actions is 17% and third parties report injection bypasses [read/snippet]. Treat the mentor as one layer, with permissions enforced outside any LLM (consistent with an earlier unpublished analysis by Hiina's agents of oversight for agents' privileged actions, which drew on auto mode's published classifier figures).
